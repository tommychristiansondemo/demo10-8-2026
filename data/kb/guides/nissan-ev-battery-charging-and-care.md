# Nissan LEAF and ARIYA: battery warranty, charging and battery care

Sources: 2023-2025 Nissan LEAF Warranty Information Booklets and LEAF Owner's Manuals (nissanusa.com), Nissan USA ARIYA
published specifications. For model-specific procedures see the vehicle's Owner's Manual.

## Battery warranty
- Lithium-ion battery (defects): **8 years / 100,000 miles**.
- Battery **capacity** coverage: **8 years / 100,000 miles** - if the LEAF battery capacity level gauge drops **below 9
  bars (of 12)** within the period, Nissan repairs or replaces the battery to restore at least 9 bars. Gradual capacity
  loss above that threshold is normal and not covered. ARIYA has the same 8-year/100,000-mile battery coverage, with
  capacity coverage defined in its own warranty booklet (third-party summaries describe the threshold as roughly 70% of original capacity).
- EV system (motor, inverter, onboard charger, etc.): 5 years / 60,000 miles.
- Coverage transfers to subsequent owners for the remaining time/mileage.

## Charging connectors
- **LEAF (2018-2025):** J1772 for Level 1/Level 2 AC charging and **CHAdeMO** for DC fast charging. CHAdeMO stations are
  becoming less common; plan routes with the NissanConnect EV app or PlugShare.
- **ARIYA:** J1772 AC charging and **CCS1** DC fast charging (up to about 130 kW on most trims). Starting 2025 Nissan EV
  owners gained access to Tesla Superchargers (NACS) using a **NACS-to-CCS1 adapter**; check the Nissan/NissanConnect app
  for adapter availability and compatible stations.
- **2026 LEAF (3rd generation):** built-in **NACS** charging port for DC fast charging at Tesla Superchargers, plus J1772 for AC.
- Level 1 (120 V household outlet) adds roughly 3-5 miles of range per hour; Level 2 (240 V) adds roughly 15-30 miles per
  hour depending on the onboard charger; DC fast charging typically reaches about 80% in 30-60 minutes depending on model,
  battery temperature and station power.

## Battery care tips (2024 LEAF Owner's Manual, "Li-ion battery life")
Battery capacity decreases with age and use; reduced range compared with new is normal and expected. To maximize battery life Nissan recommends:
- Avoid exposing the vehicle to extreme ambient temperatures for extended periods; park in cool locations out of direct sunlight.
- Avoid storing the vehicle below -13 F (-25 C) for more than seven days (the battery may freeze and be unable to charge or power the car).
- Avoid leaving the vehicle for more than 14 days with the available-charge gauge at or near zero.
- Allow the vehicle and battery to cool down after use before charging; avoid sustained high battery temperatures (for
  example extended highway driving with multiple quick charges on hot days).
- Use normal (Level 2) or trickle (Level 1) charging and minimize use of public DC fast/quick chargers.
- Moderate driving and use of ECO mode.
- Don't operate the charging timer repeatedly with the connector attached after charging completes (it can discharge the 12-volt battery).
- When temperatures may drop below -4 F (-20 C), leave the car plugged in with the power switch OFF so the battery warmer (if equipped) runs from external power.
- LEAF requires special Li-ion battery inspections at the intervals in the EV maintenance schedule; skipping them can limit warranty coverage.

## Maintenance differences
EVs have no engine oil, spark plugs or transmission fluid changes. Typical items are tire rotation, brake fluid, cabin
filter, reduction-gear oil inspection, coolant for the battery/inverter system, and an annual battery report at a Nissan
dealer (LEAF Li-ion battery check). See the model's maintenance schedule.
